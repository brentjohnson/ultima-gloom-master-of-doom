# Working Title: *Cardbearer* (placeholder)
### A retro-styled overhead RPG — pitch document

---

## One-Line Pitch

An Ultima III–style overhead RPG where you explore a fixed, hand-crafted world solo, fighting through Gloomhaven-style tactical card combat, collecting ability cards with randomized Diablo-esque properties instead of leveling up a class — in a world where danger is placed by design, not scaled to you.

---

## Core Fantasy

You are a lone adventurer walking a *real, fixed, memorizable world* — the kind you could draw a map of on graph paper, like Ultima III or Tunnels of Doom. There is no invisible hand adjusting monsters to your level. If you wander into the wrong ruin with a weak deck, it will kill you, and that's fair, because the game told you where the wrong ruins are (through art, rumor, terrain, and consequence — not floating numbers). Your power doesn't come from a class or a talent tree; it comes from the deck of ability cards you've assembled, each a small mechanical toy with its own randomized quirks, à la Diablo affixes.

---

## Pillars

1. **The map is the game.** No procedural generation of the overworld. Hand-designed regions with real geography, real secrets, and a real sense of "I remember what's over that ridge." Fixed difficulty by *place*, not by player level.
2. **Combat is a puzzle, not a slot machine.** Gloomhaven-inspired card-based combat: you build a hand from your deck, plan simultaneous-ish turns using initiative values printed on the cards, manage a limited hand/stamina economy, and win mostly through positioning and sequencing. A Gloomhaven-style attack modifier deck adds swing to each attack, but there are no to-hit rolls.
3. **Your build IS your loot.** There are no classes and no XP-into-talent-trees. Every ability you have is a physical card you found, bought, or earned. Cards can drop with randomized bonus properties (extra range, added elemental damage, reduced cost, secondary effects) — so two "Fireball" cards are never quite identical. Deckbuilding *is* character building.
4. **Hard is hard. Easy is easy.** No scaling, ever. The graveyard on the edge of town is always a curb-stomp for a fresh character and always a cakewalk once you're strong. The player's sense of growth comes from *the world staying still while they get stronger* — the Ultima/Souls approach, not the WoW "everything conveniently matches your level" approach.
5. **Optional stakes.** Hardcore mode (permadeath) is a toggle, not a default. Normal mode lets you learn the world and its dangers without a single mistake ending the save file.

## Genre Touchstones & What We're Borrowing

| Influence | What we're taking |

| **Dungeon Master (Amiga)** | Tense, resource-scarce dungeon crawling; dungeons as tighter, darker, trap-laden versions of the same overhead view (not a separate mode) |
| **Tunnels of Doom (TI-99/4A)** | The feeling of a small, personal, systemic RPG where exploration and combat are distinct but tightly linked |

| **World of Warcraft** | Rich, lived-in world lore; sense of a persistent place worth exploring |
| **Gloomhaven** | Hand-management combat, cards as both your abilities and your resource pool, initiative-driven tactical turns |

## Core Loop

1. **Explore** the fixed overworld on foot, discovering towns, dungeons, and wilderness dangers exactly where the designers put them.
2. **Fight** using your card hand — draw/manage a limited set of ability cards each encounter, play them for effects and initiative, manage stamina/exhaustion Gloomhaven-style.
3. **Loot** cards and gear from defeated enemies and dungeon treasure — some are new cards, some are cards you already own but with different randomized properties.
4. **Curate your deck** — deckbuilding *is* leveling up. Swap in stronger or more synergistic cards, retire weak ones, chase the perfect roll on a favorite ability.
5. **Push into harder fixed zones** once your deck (not your character level) can handle it.

---

## Character Progression (No Classes)

- No class selection at creation. You start with a small, humble starter deck.
- Growth = **deck growth**, not stat growth on a level-up screen. Finding/buying/earning new cards *is* your progression system.
- Some baseline stats (health, stamina capacity, carry weight) may still creep up slowly through use or discovery — but the headline power curve is entirely about *which cards you hold and how good their rolled properties are.*
- This means two players' characters can look wildly different at the "same" point in the game, based on what they found and chose to specialize in — closer to a roguelike deckbuilder's build diversity than a traditional RPG class fantasy.

---

## Loot & Randomization

- **Ability cards drop as loot.** A "Cleave" card isn't just a card — it's a card with a randomly rolled bonus property (e.g., +1 range, bleeds on hit, reduced stamina cost, "no discard after use" this time).
- Rarity tiers exist (as in Diablo) — common cards roll small bonuses, rare cards roll bigger or stranger ones.
- Non-card equipment (weapons, armor, trinkets) still exists for baseline stats and flavor, but the *build-defining* randomness lives in the cards themselves.
- No affix power creep tied to player level — a rare card found in the starting village and a rare card found in the endgame ruin are balanced by *where they are*, not by a level-scaled loot table.

---

## Combat

- Overhead view is preserved even in dungeons. Dungeons are simply tighter, darker, trap-and-puzzle-dense versions of the same map style.
- Fights happen in place on the world map: the tiles around you become the battlefield, and the UI shifts into card play. Each round you commit one card, with its initiative and stamina cost, and everyone acts in initiative order. Stamina carries between fights and fully refills only at a rest, so it is a hard resource limit (not mana regen).
- No to-hit rolls — attacks draw from a Gloomhaven-style modifier deck (+1, -1, x2, null and so on), so outcomes come mostly from card choice, positioning, and sequencing, with a bounded, visible swing.
- Enemy difficulty is hand-placed per zone/dungeon and never scales to the player.

---

## World Structure

- A hand-authored overworld map, Ultima III-style: towns, wilderness, dungeons, all fixed in place and difficulty.
- Difficulty is communicated through diegetic and environmental cues (terrain, monster visuals, NPC warnings, ruined equipment lying around) rather than a level number over an enemy's head.
- Resting resets zones: enemies return to the same hand-placed spots, so drops can be farmed while danger stays fixed. Unique bosses and treasure stay claimed.
- Early "safe ring" around the starting town, with progressively rougher regions the farther out (or the more off-the-beaten-path) you go — but always *fixed*, so a returning player feels their own growth against a static yardstick.

---

## Stakes & Modes

- **Standard Mode:** Death has a penalty (lost gold, item durability, etc.) but the character and deck persist.
- **Hardcore Mode (optional toggle):** Permadeath. Character and deck are gone on death, à la Diablo Hardcore. For players who want the fixed-difficulty philosophy to have real teeth.

---

## Tone & Setting

Lighter high fantasy in the Ultima tradition — virtue, exploration, and a sense of wonder, with real danger and dread in the right places rather than constant grimdark misery. Whimsical towns and NPCs, but ruins and wilds that are genuinely, unapologetically dangerous. The tone contrast (cozy hub towns vs. deadly fixed dungeons) is a feature, not a bug — it's what makes "hard areas are hard" feel meaningful rather than punishing.
