# Working Title: *Cardbearer* (placeholder)
### A retro-styled overhead RPG — pitch document

*This document says what the game is and why it's worth making. For how it works, see [game-design.md](game-design.md).*

---

## One-Line Pitch

An Ultima III–style overhead RPG where you explore a fixed, hand-crafted world solo, fighting through Gloomhaven-style tactical card combat, collecting ability cards with randomized Diablo-esque properties and leveling up without a class — in a world where danger is placed by design, not scaled to you.

---

## Core Fantasy

You are a lone adventurer walking a *real, fixed, memorizable world* — the kind you could draw a map of on graph paper, like Ultima III or Tunnels of Doom. There is no invisible hand adjusting monsters to your level. If you wander into the wrong ruin too early, it will kill you, and that's fair, because the game told you where the wrong ruins are (through art, rumor, terrain, and consequence — not floating numbers). You choose who you become: each level lets you lean toward fighting, spellcasting, or a balance of both, and the deck you assemble does the rest, each card a small mechanical toy with its own randomized quirks, à la Diablo affixes.

---

## Pillars

1. **The map is the game.** No procedural generation of the overworld. Hand-designed regions with real geography, real secrets, and a real sense of "I remember what's over that ridge." Fixed difficulty by *place*, not by player level.
2. **Combat is a puzzle, not a slot machine.** Gloomhaven-inspired card-based combat: plan each turn around the initiative printed on your cards, manage your resources, and win mostly through positioning and sequencing. An attack modifier deck adds bounded swing, but there are no to-hit rolls.
3. **Your build is yours.** There are no classes and no talent trees. Your abilities are cards you found, bought, or earned, and no two "Fireball" cards are quite identical. Leveling up lets you shape what the deck can't: how much you can endure, and how good your always-available basic actions are.
4. **Hard is hard. Easy is easy.** No scaling, ever. The graveyard on the edge of town is always a curb-stomp for a fresh character and always a cakewalk once you're strong. The player's sense of growth comes from *the world staying still while they get stronger* — the Ultima/Souls approach, not the WoW "everything conveniently matches your level" approach.
5. **Optional stakes.** Hardcore mode (permadeath) is a toggle, not a default. Normal mode lets you learn the world and its dangers without a single mistake ending the save file.

## Genre Touchstones & What We're Borrowing

| Influence | What we're taking |
|---|---|
| **Ultima III** | Overhead tile-based movement, fixed hand-authored world map, town/dungeon/wilderness structure |
| **Dungeon Master (Amiga)** | Tense, resource-scarce dungeon crawling; dungeons as tighter, darker, trap-laden versions of the same overhead view (not a separate mode) |
| **Tunnels of Doom (TI-99/4A)** | The feeling of a small, personal, systemic RPG where exploration and combat are distinct but tightly linked |
| **Diablo** | Random item/ability drops, satisfying loot chase, optional Hardcore permadeath |
| **World of Warcraft** | Rich, lived-in world lore; sense of a persistent place worth exploring |
| **Gloomhaven** | Card-driven combat, initiative-driven tactical turns, attack modifier deck |

## Core Loop

1. **Explore** the fixed overworld on foot, discovering towns, dungeons, and wilderness dangers exactly where the designers put them.
2. **Fight** with your hand of ability cards and always-available basic actions, on the same tiles you were just walking across.
3. **Loot** cards from defeated enemies and dungeon treasure, chasing better rolls on the abilities you love.
4. **Grow** by curating your deck and by leveling up, choosing whether to become more of a fighter, a caster, or both.
5. **Push into harder fixed zones** once you can handle them.

---

## Tone & Setting

Lighter high fantasy in the Ultima tradition — virtue, exploration, and a sense of wonder, with real danger and dread in the right places rather than constant grimdark misery. Whimsical towns and NPCs, but ruins and wilds that are genuinely, unapologetically dangerous. The tone contrast (cozy hub towns vs. deadly fixed dungeons) is a feature, not a bug — it's what makes "hard areas are hard" feel meaningful rather than punishing.
